The Research Rabbit Hole

Are you researching a question, or hunting for proof?

Before you begin

Welcome, researcher.

Imagine you're beginning a project about teenagers and social media. You have an opinion already. Your task is to investigate the issue, not simply gather articles that agree with you.

You'll make research decisions in three rounds. There are no secret “correct” articles. You'll receive feedback only after you finish, so you can see how your thinking develops.

Your starting belief:
“Social media is harmful to teenagers.”

The article titles and descriptions in this simulation are fictional teaching examples, not citations to real published studies. No account or internet connection is required. Your progress stays in this browser until you reset it.

Round 1 · Your first search

Which three results would you open first?

You search a scholarly database and find six promising article descriptions. Select exactly three that you would read first. Choose based on your current thinking, not on what you think your instructor wants.

Starting belief: “Social media is harmful to teenagers.”

0 of 3 selected

Round 2 · The picture gets complicated

The research raises more questions.

As you read, you encounter studies that approach social media from different angles. Some examine possible harms, while others explore circumstances in which experiences differ.

Choose two questions you think researchers should investigate next. You're not deciding which conclusions are true.

0 of 2 questions selected

Round 3 · Rethink the investigation

Would you research differently now?

Return to your starting belief and consider how your research priorities may have changed.

Which statement best describes your thinking now?

Aim for a question you could investigate without assuming the answer.
